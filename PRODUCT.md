# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Local homeowners, roughly 35 to 65, in Berks, Montgomery, and Chester Counties, PA (within about 30 miles of Douglassville). Most arrive on a phone from Google search or the Google Business Profile, often comparing two or three contractors. They want a new deck, a replacement for a worn or unsafe one, or a pool deck. The job to be done: decide whether Turner's Decks is trustworthy and affordable enough to call, then request a free on-site estimate (quote form or phone). The price estimator is a secondary path for people who want a ballpark number first.

## Product Purpose

The marketing site for Turner's Decks, a small owner-run deck builder (Pat Turner, since 2018). It exists to turn local search traffic into estimate requests and phone calls. Success is a steady flow of qualified leads from the quote form, estimator, and tap-to-call, and visitors leaving with the sense that this crew does real work and tells you straight what it costs.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, craftsman, local. A plainspoken tradesman who shows the work instead of talking about it. The voice is direct and specific (real towns, real materials, real permit steps), never salesy. The site should make a homeowner feel confident ("these people know what they're doing"), at ease ("they'll handle the permits"), and like they're dealing with a neighbor, not a franchise. The finished carpentry in real project photos is the hero.

## Anti-references

- Franchise or big-box: Trex, Archadeck, Home Depot installer pages. Corporate stock photography and national-brand polish.
- Generic contractor templates: WordPress trades themes, orange hard hats, icon grids, "Quality Service Guaranteed" badges, fake trust seals.
- SaaS or startup gloss: gradients, glassmorphism, big stat counters, anything that reads "tech company".
- Luxury showroom: overly precious, magazine-luxe styling that signals "expensive" and scares off the average homeowner.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the work.** Real project photos and specifics beat claims. If a section can be a photo or a concrete fact, it shouldn't be an icon and a slogan.
2. **Plain talk earns trust.** Say what it costs, what's included, and who does the work. No filler, no superlatives.
3. **One clear next step.** Every page leads to a free estimate or a phone call, reachable with one thumb on a phone.
4. **Neighbor, not franchise.** Local names, towns, and the owner's presence over corporate polish.
5. **Honest craft over decoration.** Sturdy and well-made, like the decks: nothing ornamental that doesn't help someone decide.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA minimum. Many visitors are 50 to 70: body text at least 16px with comfortable line height, strong contrast (avoid light gray on white), tap targets at least 44px, phone number always easy to find and tap. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Don't rely on color alone (e.g. estimator states, form errors). Forms need visible labels and clear, plain-language error messages.
